Words

Words

The common ones, first, each with what it is for. A few more sit at the end: numbers, family, food, weather, and the way you ask for help. Standard spelling. Not a full dictionary.

  • eu I. The person speaking.
  • tu You, to one person you know. Use dumneavoastră with a stranger or an elder.
  • el He. Also “it” for a masculine thing.
  • ea She. Also “it” for a feminine thing.
  • noi We.
  • voi You, to more than one person.
  • ei They. Ei for a masculine or mixed group. Ele for a feminine group.
  • a fi To be. Eu sunt, tu ești, el este. The verb under almost every sentence.
  • a avea To have. Also used for age: am douăzeci de ani, I am twenty.
  • a face To do, or to make. Ce faci? is the ordinary “what are you doing,” and also a hello.
  • a merge To go, on foot or in general. Mergem acasă, we are going home.
  • a veni To come. The opposite of a merge. Vino aici, come here.
  • a vedea To see. Am văzut marea, I saw the sea.
  • a ști To know a fact, or to know how. Not the same as a cunoaște, which is to be acquainted with a person or a place.
  • a vrea To want. Vreau apă, I want water.
  • a putea Can, to be able. Nu pot, I cannot.
  • a spune To say, to tell. Spune-mi, tell me.
  • a da To give. Also shows up in please: vă rog is a different verb, but a da is the giving itself.
  • a lua To take. Ia pâinea, take the bread.
  • a mânca To eat. Mănânc, I eat. The noun is mâncare.
  • a bea To drink. Beau apă, I drink water.
  • a dormi To sleep. Dorm, I sleep. Noapte bună is the good-night you say before it.
  • da Yes. Short and enough.
  • nu No. Also the “not” in front of a verb: nu știu, I do not know.
  • și And.
  • sau Or.
  • dar But.
  • că That, as in “I know that…” Știu că e bine.
  • cu With. Cu sare, with salt.
  • fără Without.
  • în In, inside. În casă, in the house.
  • pe On, and also the marker in front of a person you act on. Văd pe mama, I see mother.
  • la At, or to. La masă, at the table. La mare, to the sea.
  • din From, out of. Din sat, from the village.
  • pentru For. Pentru tine, for you.
  • acest This. Acest drum, this road. Această for a feminine noun.
  • acela That, farther off. Same idea as the English “that one.”
  • aici Here.
  • acolo There.
  • acum Now.
  • azi Today. Also written astăzi.
  • ieri Yesterday.
  • mâine Tomorrow.
  • bine Well, or all right. A reply to ce faci? is often bine.
  • rău Bad, or badly. Îmi pare rău, I am sorry.
  • mare Big. The same spelling is also the sea. Context tells you which.
  • mic Small.
  • bun Good. Pâine bună, good bread. Bună is also the short hello.
  • nou New.
  • vechi Old, of a thing. For an old person, bătrân.
  • mult Much, a lot. Multă apă, a lot of water.
  • puțin A little. Puțină sare, a little salt.
  • om A person, a human being. Also “man” in the general sense.
  • femeie Woman.
  • bărbat Man.
  • copil Child.
  • mamă Mother. Mama is “mom,” the word you actually say.
  • tată Father. Tata is “dad.”
  • casă House, or home. Acasă means at home.
  • sat Village.
  • oraș City, or town.
  • apă Water. From Latin aqua. The thing you carry from the well.
  • pâine Bread. The ordinary food of the table — more on bread.
  • mâncare Food. What you eat, not the act of eating.
  • vin Wine. The household glass — more on wine.
  • lapte Milk.
  • sare Salt.
  • zi Day. Bună ziua is the polite hello, any hour you are unsure.
  • noapte Night.
  • dimineață Morning.
  • seară Evening. Seara bună is the evening greeting.
  • an Year.
  • timp Time, and also weather. Cât timp, how long.
  • lucru A thing, and also work. Am de lucru, I have work to do.
  • mână Hand.
  • cap Head. Also the end of a thing, as in the head of the table.
  • ochi Eye. The plural is the same shape you hear in a pair.
  • inima The heart. Inimă without the article. Used for courage as well as the organ.
  • nume Name. Cum te cheamă? is how you ask it, not “what is your name” word for word.
  • cuvânt Word.
  • drum Road, or the way. Pe drum, on the road.
  • munte Mountain. Munții, the mountains, are the Carpathians in most of these pages.
  • râu River. Not the same as mare, the sea.
  • cer Sky, and also heaven, depending on the sentence.
  • soare Sun.
  • lumină Light.
  • biserică Church. The building, and often the parish around it.
  • ușă Door.
  • masă Table, and also a meal. La masă means at the table, sitting down to eat.
  • scaun Chair.
  • prieten Friend. Prietenă for a woman.
  • dragoste Love. Heavier than “like.” Te iubesc is the sentence people actually say.
  • pace Peace. Also the ordinary wish after a quarrel, and a church word.
  • frică Fear. Mi-e frică, I am afraid.
  • bucurie Joy. A warm word, not a joke-word.
  • a întreba To ask. Întreb, I ask.
  • a răspunde To answer. Răspunde-mi, answer me.
  • a rămâne To stay, to remain. Rămân aici, I am staying here.
  • a pleca To leave. Plec, I am leaving.
  • a deschide To open.
  • a închide To close.
  • a aștepta To wait. Așteaptă, wait.
  • a găsi To find.
  • unde Where.
  • când When.
  • cum How. Also “what” in cum te cheamă, what are you called.
  • cine Who.
  • ce What.
  • de ce Why.
  • vă rog Please. The polite form. Te rog is the same to someone you know.
  • scuzați Excuse me, to a stranger. Scuză-mă to a friend.
  • bună Hi. The short hello. Safer with people you already know.
  • bună ziua Good day. The safe hello at any hour.
  • seara bună Good evening.
  • mulțumesc Thank you. The ț is a ts sound. Add mult for many thanks.
  • poftă bună Said before a meal. Literally a good appetite. The reply is often the same, or mulțumesc.
  • noroc Luck. Also cheers, when glasses meet.
  • dor A longing for a person or a place that is not here. Not quite homesick, not quite missing.
  • la revedere Until we see each other again. The longer goodbye. Pa is the short one.
  • unu One. The first number. Un bărbat, one man. O for a feminine noun: o casă.
  • doi Two. Două with a feminine noun. Doi copii, two children.
  • trei Three.
  • zece Ten.
  • sută A hundred. O sută, one hundred.
  • frate Brother.
  • soră Sister.
  • bunic Grandfather. Bunică is grandmother. The words people say are often bunicu and bunica.
  • bunică Grandmother. The person in the well story who knows where the spring is.
  • carne Meat.
  • pește Fish.
  • brânză Cheese. Telemea is the salty white kind used in the egg recipe.
  • ou Egg. Ouă is the plural. The Easter craft starts here.
  • cafea Coffee.
  • ceai Tea.
  • zahăr Sugar.
  • picior Leg, or foot, depending on the sentence.
  • gură Mouth.
  • ploaie Rain. Plouă, it is raining.
  • vânt Wind.
  • cald Warm, or hot. E cald, it is warm.
  • frig Cold. Mi-e frig, I am cold.
  • a cumpăra To buy. Cumpăr pâine, I buy bread.
  • a vinde To sell.
  • a lucra To work. Lucrez, I work. Not the same as lucru, the noun.
  • a citi To read.
  • a scrie To write.
  • a înțelege To understand. Înțeleg, I understand. Nu înțeleg, I do not.
  • stradă Street.
  • piață Market, or a square in town. Where you buy the tomatoes.
  • școală School.
  • săptămână Week.
  • lună Month, and also the moon.
  • oră Hour, and also a class at school. O oră, one hour.
  • bani Money. Plural in ordinary speech.
  • ajutor Help. Ajutor! is the shout. A ajuta is the verb.
  • dreapta The right side. La dreapta, to the right.
  • stânga The left side. La stânga.
  • astăzi Today, the longer spelling of azi. Both are used.
  • noapte bună Good night. Said when someone is going to sleep.
