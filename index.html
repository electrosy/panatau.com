Romania

Pictures, not a catalog.

History

Short lessons. Not a textbook, and not a single hero story.

Lesson 1

The Latin that stayed

Read the lesson

Before Rome, the Carpathian basin held the Dacian kingdom. After Trajan’s wars in the early 100s, Dacia became a Roman province for a relatively short time. What lasted was not the administration. It was a Latin-based language, still spoken when the empire was gone. Romanian is the eastern cousin of Italian, French, and Spanish. Slavonic, Hungarian, Turkish, and Greek left words, not the grammar.

Lesson 2

Three lands, not one court

Read the lesson

For most of the last thousand years there was no single Romanian state. Wallachia lay south of the mountains, Moldavia to the east, Transylvania inside the arc and usually under a Hungarian or Habsburg crown. Princes paid tribute, switched protectors, and built churches as a way to stay visible. When you see a painted monastery or a wooden gate, you are looking at that arrangement, not at a national museum planned in advance.

Lesson 3

Why the walls are painted

Read the lesson

In northern Moldavia, exterior frescoes were a public book for people who might not read. Saints, sieges, and the last judgment sit on the outside wall, in the weather. Voroneț blue is famous because the pigment held. The lesson is practical: the church was a classroom and a landmark, not only a shrine.

Lesson 4

How the country was put together

Read the lesson

In 1859 Wallachia and Moldavia chose the same prince, Alexandru Ioan Cuza. That personal union is the start of the modern state. Transylvania joined after the First World War, in 1918, when the old empires broke. The map people recognize today is young next to the ridges. Borders moved again in the 1940s. Hold that in mind when an old story names a town: the town may have changed crowns more than once.

Lesson 5

A century, read slowly

Read the lesson

The twentieth century here is not a clean arc. There was a kingdom, a dictatorship, alliance with and then against the Axis, a communist state, and a violent end to that state in 1989. No one portrait covers it. A useful habit: ask who is speaking, what they stood to lose, and what the church, the village, or the city street was doing at the same time. The pictures on this site are not evidence. They are a way to stay with the place while you read further.

Lesson 6

Thrace and Dacia

Read the lesson

Two old names, often paired and not the same kingdom. One language left almost nothing. The other is tied to the Latin that stayed north of the Danube.

Lesson 7

Constanța

Read the lesson

Tomis to the Greeks, a Roman exile’s city, an Ottoman harbor, and since 1878 Romania’s main window on the Black Sea.

Recipes

Fourteen common ones, kept simple. Home versions, not a canon.

Mămăligă

Serves 4

  • 1 cup coarse cornmeal
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • A spoon of butter, if you want it softer

Bring the water and salt to a boil. Rain in the cornmeal while you stir so it does not clump. Lower the heat and stir often until it pulls from the side of the pot, about 20 minutes. Rest 5 minutes. Slice with a wet knife or a string. Eat with cheese, eggs, or mujdei.

Mujdei

A small bowl

  • 4 garlic cloves
  • A pinch of salt
  • 3 tablespoons oil, or the same of water
  • A squeeze of lemon, optional

Crush the garlic with the salt until it is a paste. Stir in the oil a little at a time, or use water for a sharper sauce. Lemon if you like it bright. It should be loose enough to spoon, not a mayonnaise. Good on potatoes, grilled meat, or mămăligă.

Salată de varză

Serves 4

  • Half a small white cabbage, sliced thin
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar
  • A little dill, if you have it

Toss the cabbage with the salt and leave it 10 minutes. Squeeze out the extra water. Stir in oil, vinegar, and dill. Taste. It should be sharp and a bit wet, not a dry slaw. Better after a short rest in the cold.

Ouă cu telemea

Serves 2

  • 3 eggs
  • A handful of telemea, or any salty white cheese
  • 2 green onions, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon butter or oil
  • Salt only if the cheese is mild

Warm the fat in a small pan. Add the onions for a minute. Crumble in the cheese, then pour in the beaten eggs. Cook low, folding once, until just set. Telemea is already salty, so taste before you add salt.

Tocăniță de cartofi

Serves 4

  • 6 medium potatoes, cubed
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 teaspoon paprika
  • Salt, and a little dill

Soften the onion in the oil. Stir in the paprika for a few seconds, then the potatoes and salt. Add just enough water to come halfway up. Cover and simmer until the potatoes are soft and the liquid is mostly gone, about 25 minutes. Dill at the end. This is weekday food, not a feast.

Fasole bătută

Serves 4

  • 2 cups cooked white beans, or one large can, drained
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 3 tablespoons oil
  • Salt, and a little onion

Mash the warm beans with the garlic and oil until they are thick but not dry. Salt it. A raw onion on the side is traditional and optional. Eat with bread, pickles, or mămăligă. If the beans are canned, rinse them first so the mash is not tinny.

Salată de roșii

Serves 4

  • 4 ripe tomatoes, sliced
  • 1 small onion, sliced thin
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • Salt, and a little vinegar if you like

Salt the tomatoes and onion and leave them a few minutes so they make their own juice. Add oil. That is the salad. Summer only, and only if the tomatoes taste like something. It sits next to almost every hot dish on this page.

Pilaf de orez

Serves 4

  • 1 cup rice, rinsed
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons oil or butter
  • 2 cups water or light stock
  • Salt

Soften the onion in the fat. Add the rice and stir until the grains look glossy. Pour in the liquid and salt, bring to a simmer, cover, and cook low until the water is gone, about 15 minutes. Rest off the heat, then fluff. Onion rice, not a spiced biryani.

Ciorbă de legume

Serves 4

  • 1 carrot, 1 parsley root or parsnip, 1 onion
  • 2 potatoes, cubed
  • A handful of cabbage or green beans
  • 1 tablespoon oil, salt, lovage or parsley
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar, or the juice of half a lemon

Chop the roots small. Soften the onion in the oil, add the other vegetables and enough water to cover by two fingers. Salt and simmer until the potato is soft, about 25 minutes. Take it off the heat and sour it with vinegar. The sour note is the point. Lovage if you can find it; parsley will do.

Sarmale

Serves 4

  • 12 pickled cabbage leaves, or blanched fresh ones
  • 250 g ground pork or mixed meat
  • ½ cup rice, rinsed
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • Salt, pepper, a little dill, tomato sauce to cover

Soften the onion, mix with the meat, raw rice, salt, pepper, and dill. Put a spoon of filling on each leaf and roll, tucking the sides. Pack them in a pot, cover with diluted tomato sauce and a little water, and simmer covered about an hour until the rice is soft. Home size, not a wedding tray.

Ardei umpluți

Serves 4

  • 4 large bell peppers, tops cut and seeds out
  • 250 g ground meat, or lentils for a meatless day
  • ½ cup rice, rinsed
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • Tomato sauce, salt, pepper

Mix the meat, rice, onion, salt, and pepper. Stuff the peppers loosely — the rice swells. Stand them in a pot, pour tomato sauce and water to come halfway up, and simmer covered until the peppers are soft and the rice is done, about 45 minutes. Same idea as sarmale, in a pepper.

Salată de vinete

Serves 4

  • 2 large eggplants
  • 1 small onion, chopped fine, or a spoon of mayonnaise
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • Salt, and a squeeze of lemon

Roast or grill the eggplants until the skin is black and the flesh collapses. Peel while warm, drain in a colander, then chop or mash. Stir in oil, salt, lemon, and onion (or mayonnaise if that is how your house does it). Chill. Spread on bread. Smoky and plain.

Zacuscă

A jar for the week

  • 2 eggplants and 2 red peppers, roasted and peeled
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • Salt, and a pinch of sugar if the paste is sharp

Chop the roasted vegetables small. Soften the onions in the oil, add the paste, then the eggplant and peppers. Cook low, stirring, until it thickens and darkens, 20–30 minutes. Salt to taste. Cool and keep in the fridge. A spread for bread, not a side salad.

Papanași

Serves 4

  • 250 g fresh cheese (urdă or cottage cheese, drained)
  • 1 egg
  • 3–4 tablespoons flour, plus more for dusting
  • A pinch of salt and sugar
  • Sour cream and jam to serve; oil for frying

Mix the cheese, egg, flour, salt, and a little sugar into a soft dough. Shape small discs with a hole, like fat doughnuts. Dust with flour and fry in shallow hot oil until golden on both sides. Drain. Serve warm with sour cream and jam. Weekend food, not everyday.

Words

A few you can actually use. Spelled as they are written.

A hundred common words · Stories in Romanian · Translate · Learn Romanian · Learn English

Bună ziua

Good day. The safe hello, any hour you are unsure. Say the last word like ZEE-wah.

Mulțumesc

Thank you. The ț is a ts sound. Mool-TSOO-mesk is close enough to be understood.

Poftă bună

Said before a meal, the way you would say enjoy. Literally, a good appetite.

Noroc

Luck. Used as cheers when glasses meet. Also a wish when someone starts something.

Dor

A longing for a person or a place that is not here. Not quite homesick, not quite missing. The site is a little of this. Courtship that used that word is on romance.

La revedere

Until we see each other again. The longer goodbye. Pa is the short one, with people you already know.
